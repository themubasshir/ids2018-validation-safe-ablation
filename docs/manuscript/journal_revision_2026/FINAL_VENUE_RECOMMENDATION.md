## Why JISA

The manuscript's contribution is a controlled empirical audit of ML-based intrusion-detection evaluation: temporal validation, shortcut/placebo controls, bidirectional transfer, operating-point behavior, workload projections, hardware profiling, held-out-family testing, seed stability, and provenance limits. It does not claim a new IDS architecture.

JISA's official scope explicitly covers original research and practice-driven applications relevant to information security. Its public author guide provides concrete requirements compatible with the revision: 250-word maximum abstract, 1–7 keywords, editable source submission, figures/tables, supplementary material, declarations, funding, conflicts, data availability, and generative-AI disclosure. Its hybrid route preserves a subscription path, subject to current portal confirmation.

## Why not the other finalists

- **Computers & Security:** excluded. Current Elsevier scope/guide states an AI/ML moratorium covering submissions where AI/ML is a significant component, including applying AI/ML to system security/privacy. This manuscript audits ML intrusion detection; no applicable exception is stated.
- **ACM TOPS:** strongest ambitious alternative. Its scope explicitly includes evaluation, auditing, and intrusion detection. It ranks below JISA because ACM's originality threshold, author-review requirements, page limit, and current gold-OA cost create greater preparation and funding risk for a broad audit contribution.
- **Computer Networks:** excellent alternative because official scope explicitly includes network security and intrusion detection. It ranks below JISA because the manuscript is not principally a networking protocol, architecture, or network-systems paper; application framing would need strengthening.
- **JNCA:** strong alternative, but the manuscript would need a more explicit network-application/deployment contribution.
- **IEEE TDSC/TIFS:** prestigious and credible, but TDSC requires stronger dependable-systems framing; TIFS has a strong forensics/signal orientation. Neither is as direct a fit as JISA.
- **Journal of Cybersecurity:** OUP requires interdisciplinary, societal, human, organizational, or broad societal significance. The current technical manuscript does not clearly satisfy the stated in-scope threshold.
- **Cybersecurity / IJIS:** scientifically plausible, but fully OA cost and current indexing/ranking verification remain less favorable than JISA's documented route.
