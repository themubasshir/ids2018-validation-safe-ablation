# 4. Validation Framework and Methods

## 4.1 Reference Evaluation and Governance

The reference analysis used the frozen processed CSE-CIC-IDS2018 population described in Section 3, with 78 numeric predictors and fixed stratified train, validation, and holdout memberships. Stages 1–5 define the reference protocol. Stage 1 excluded `Label` and `binary_label`, retained the frozen memberships, and fitted `StandardScaler` only on training data for learners requiring scaling; tree-based learners used their prescribed unscaled representation. The historical split seed was 42. These operations were implemented in the archived notebook cells 93–107 and specified in `configs/stage01/protocol.json` through `configs/stage05/protocol.json`.

Stage 2 supplied the baseline learner inventory, including the recorded classical, boosting, and neural constructors. Stage 3 defined validation-only hyperparameter searches and candidate grids. Stage 4 selected operating points from validation predictions using the frozen integer-percent threshold grid and objective-specific tie-breaking rules; the holdout was not used for threshold selection. Stage 5 applied the selected operating points once to the frozen holdout and reported discrimination and threshold metrics. The native metric set includes ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, F1, F2, FPR, calibration/Brier quantities, and confusion-derived operating measures where supported by the relevant protocol.

Stages 8–12 extended governance without creating a single universal uncertainty procedure. Stage 8 used paired, class-stratified bootstrap comparisons for selected reference contrasts. Stage 9 described calibration without recalibration of the evaluated model. Stage 10 selected an operational cost-ratio operating point on validation data; its operating-cost interpretation is conditional on the declared cost assumptions. Stage 11 separated known-category evaluation from unseen-family testing and retained category-support restrictions. Stage 12 assessed fixed-recipe sensitivity across a finite seed set. None of these reference results establishes temporal validity, cross-dataset transfer, deployment utility, or unseen-attack-family generalization.

The reference condition is an externally supplied processed population. It therefore remains distinct from the later source-faithful temporal populations and from transfer, prevalence, and LOAO populations. Approved reference evidence is routed to Supplement S1 and Tables S1; Stage 29 identifiers such as `E29-001` are evidence-registry identifiers, not supplement section names.

## 4.2 Temporal Validation

Temporal validation addressed forward ranking and operating-point transfer using the source-faithful Stage22R protocol, with supporting temporal feasibility work in Stage18 and seed/control extensions in Stage28. The effective Stage22R design was a precommitted 2 x 2 factorial: RANDOM or CHRONOLOGICAL development splitting crossed with NATURAL or training-only REBALANCED class composition. Chronological development trained on 14–23 February source days and validated on 28 February. Random development used a stratified 80/20 split of the same K79-clean development universe. Rebalancing was deterministic benign undersampling applied only to training; validation and final evaluation were not resampled.

K79 was defined as the corrected integer-second timestamp plus the 78 common numeric predictors, with the label excluded. Predictor tokens were parsed as float64; NaN was canonicalized, infinities remained distinct for identity, and negative zero was normalized. BLAKE2b-128 accelerated candidate matching, followed by exact canonical-record verification. Development exact conflicts and duplicates were removed before split construction. At final-holdout cleaning, retained development signatures had priority, then mixed-label holdout conflicts were excluded, followed by deterministic same-label duplicate collapse. This control establishes exact K79 non-overlap only. It does not establish S4, session, endpoint, source-IP, or five-tuple independence.

All four cells used the frozen 70-feature ordered model representation and an equal-weight LightGBM/XGBoost ensemble, `0.5 * P_LIGHTGBM + 0.5 * P_XGBOOST`, with no hyperparameter search. Standard, balanced, and security thresholds were selected from development validation only. The shared Mar1–Mar2 forward holdout was historically opened once for all four cells and is permanently closed; no holdout fitting, calibration, threshold search, feature search, or model choice was permitted. The four cells therefore share an evaluation geometry, but chronology and attack-family composition are not independently identified causal factors.

Stage18 supplied earlier temporal representation-feasibility evidence, including an audit of seven ViT representations with zero passing the registered feasibility criterion; no Stage18 ViT model was trained under that rejected protocol. Stage19 used strict one-second and multiscale forward chronology, so its SingleScale and MTemporal experiments are not directly comparable to per-flow Stage15/16 experiments. These branches are retained as methodological and supplement evidence rather than merged into the Stage22R estimand.

The primary sources are `notebooks/archive/stage21_stage22_research_continues.ipynb`, `configs/stage22/protocol.json`, `docs/STAGE22R_MANUSCRIPT_INTEGRATION.md`, and the Stage22R membership, input, model, and holdout receipts. Temporal and representation diagnostics are routed to Supplement S2 and the relevant approved evidence exhibits.

## 4.3 Shortcut-Feature and Explanation Audit

Stages 6, 13, 14, and 23 addressed distinct questions. Stage 6 used a shared-sample dual-model TreeSHAP protocol to describe model-attribution components. Stage 13 specified a deterministic local LIME panel and SHAP–LIME agreement diagnostics. Stage 14 defined Integrated Gradients compatibility and mathematical checks for neural paths. These procedures concern explanation behavior and fidelity under their declared inputs; they do not convert attribution into causal evidence.

Stage23 was the preregistered validation-safe shortcut audit. Its canonical methodology export is `scripts/stage23_shortcut_feature_audit_kaggle.py`, with execution chronology and outputs preserved in `notebooks/archive/stage23_research_executed.ipynb`. It evaluated seven registered feature subsets on the frozen Stage22 random-natural and chronological populations, with matched-size placebo controls, stump/depth probes, proxy diagnostics, split/learner interaction checks, descriptive TreeSHAP comparisons, and a deterministic local explanation panel. The audit retained separate model and split effects and froze its fit, explanation, and uncertainty artifacts. Feature removal or attribution changes indicate sensitivity to the tested representation; they do not prove leakage, causality, or a unique transfer mechanism. Stage23 `NO_PORTS` removes `Dst Port` and `Protocol`; `Src Port` was not present and is not described as removed. Approved shortcut and representation evidence is routed to Supplement S2 and Tables S2–S3.

## 4.4 Cross-Dataset Transfer

Stage24 evaluated directional source-only transfer between IDS2018 and CICIDS2017. The directions were kept separate: IDS2018 to CICIDS2017 used the frozen CICIDS2017 target contract, while CICIDS2017 to IDS2018 used the February 28, 2018 target. Source fitting, preprocessing, and direction-specific validation thresholds were frozen before target evaluation; no target adaptation, mapping search, fuzzy matching, target-label mapping, or target-value-driven feature construction was permitted.

Two semantic contracts were retained. Bridge62 contains 62 features and excludes the eight aggregate flag fields `FIN Flag Cnt`, `SYN Flag Cnt`, `RST Flag Cnt`, `PSH Flag Cnt`, `ACK Flag Cnt`, `URG Flag Cnt`, `CWE Flag Count`, and `ECE Flag Cnt`; its published and flag-corrected rows are bitwise identical. Bridge70 contains 70 features and retains published and corrected serialization as distinct conditions. These contracts must not be conflated. The primary direction used the frozen bridge conditions and the secondary direction used source training on Monday–Wednesday, source validation on Thursday, and target evaluation on the frozen IDS2018 February 28 population.

Stage24 consumed four historical model fits and six evaluable target openings. The two planned `GROUNDED_S4` cells were cancelled before target opening because durable exact membership was unavailable; they are not failed results, and no result exists for them. Where specified, paired stratified row-bootstrap intervals used 2,000 replicates with seed 42. The authoritative sources are `configs/stage24/protocol.json`, `notebooks/archive/stage24_cross_dataset_executed.ipynb`, `scripts/stage24/stage24_cross_dataset_generalization.py`, and the semantic-bridge and publication receipts. Direction-specific transfer evidence is routed to Supplement S3 and Tables S4–S5.

## 4.5 Prevalence and Operational Stress

Stage10 supplied validation-era operational-cost selection within the reference protocol. Stage25 then translated frozen operating-point scalars analytically rather than by new prediction, fitting, or inference. The prevalence grid was `[0.1, 0.03, 0.01, 0.003, 0.001, 0.0001]`, with one million benign flows per day, two minutes per alert, analyst tiers `[1, 3, 10]`, 240 alerts per analyst per day, relative costs `C_FP = 1` and `C_FN = 100`, and the declared fixed-TPR/FPR prior-shift assumptions.

For prevalence $\pi$, fixed true-positive rate $t$ and false-positive rate $f$, the projected positive predictive value and negative predictive value were

$$\mathrm{PPV}=\frac{t\pi}{t\pi+f(1-\pi)}, \qquad \mathrm{NPV}=\frac{(1-f)(1-\pi)}{(1-t)\pi+(1-f)(1-\pi)}.$$

For traffic volume $N$, expected alerts were $N[t\pi+f(1-\pi)]$. Analyst time was obtained by multiplying alerts by the declared service time; capacity comparisons used the declared analyst tier and 240-alert daily capacity. Relative expected cost was $C_{FN}\pi(1-t)+C_{FP}(1-\pi)f$. These are deterministic prior-shift projections conditional on inherited rates and assumptions, not observations from a real Security Operations Center. Stage25 is the only stage classified as full scalar reproduction from frozen inputs. Evidence is routed to Supplement S4 and Table S6.

## 4.6 Deployment Profiling

Stage26 measured prepared-input-to-probability model paths under recorded CPU and single-Tesla-T4 conditions. The protocol covered warm and cold latency, latency distributions, throughput, memory, artifact size, backend compatibility, and batch sizes `[1, 64, 256, 1024, 8192]`. Profiles included Stage16 XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost paths, FT checkpoints, the Stage20 CNN, the Stage21 ViT, and ensembles where compatible. Earlier architecture experiments retain their own environments and are not treated as one common deployment benchmark.

The canonical execution record is `notebooks/archive/stage26_deployment_profiling_executed.ipynb`; `scripts/stage26/stage26_deployment_profiling_kaggle.py` is archival virtual source and is not a standalone executable module. Historical timings are hardware- and software-specific. Measurements begin at the prepared-input boundary and exclude packet capture, feature extraction, alert aggregation, network transport, and analyst response. Unsupported backends or paths are classified as unsupported, not as slow measurements. Methodology and approved measurements are routed to Supplement S5, Table S7, and the deployment figure evidence.

## 4.7 Leave-One-Attack-Family-Out Evaluation

Stages11, 27, and 28 distinguish known-category analysis, genuine LOAO evaluation, and stability controls. Stage27 used the frozen taxonomy `BOT`, `DDOS`, `DOS`, `AUTH_BRUTE_FORCE`, `INFILTRATION`, `PORT_SCAN`, and `WEB_ATTACK`. Five folds were eligible: `BOT`, `DDOS`, `INFILTRATION`, `PORT_SCAN`, and `WEB_ATTACK`; `DOS` and `AUTH_BRUTE_FORCE` were structurally ineligible. In each eligible fold, the held-out family had zero training and validation rows, with day-atomic chronology satisfying `TRAIN < VALIDATION < TARGET`. XGBoost and LightGBM were fitted under the CPU policy, and balanced and security thresholds were selected from known-family validation only using the frozen 0.01–0.99 grid. Target search and post-target changes were prohibited.

Ranking metrics and fixed-threshold metrics were reported separately. The inferential support gate required at least 50 positives; `Infiltration` remained descriptive at 36 positives. Accordingly, the analysis does not support a universal zero-day claim. Stage27 used 2,000-replicate stratified row bootstrap uncertainty with seed 42. Stage11 known-category results are not relabeled as LOAO evidence. Fold receipts, threshold receipts, and final metrics are the authoritative sources; evidence is routed to Supplement S6 and Table S8.

## 4.8 Seed and Control Stability

Stage12 assessed fixed-recipe reference sensitivity across its registered finite seed design. Stage28 extended stability using seeds `[42, 43, 44, 45, 46]`, frozen recipes, and separate chronology-LOAO and matched random-LOAO controls. The protocol authorized 108 new fits and 12 historical reuses, with no best-seed selection, full hyperparameter-search repetition, new significance test, or new qualitative cutoff. Stage28 evaluated the historically opened Stage22R shared holdout again under the frozen geometry; it did not create an untouched new holdout.

Seed spread and bootstrap intervals were retained as different uncertainty objects. A five-seed ordering describes sensitivity across those realizations; it is not a population guarantee or causal identification. Random-LOAO controls test matched membership behavior but do not isolate one causal mechanism. Stage28 is the final empirical wall: no further fits, target openings, threshold reselection, or significance testing are authorized. Stability evidence is routed to Supplement S6 and Table S9.

## 4.9 Statistical Uncertainty and Anti-Adaptation Controls

Uncertainty was stage-specific. Reference contrasts used the registered paired class-stratified bootstrap where applicable. Stage24 transfer contrasts used frozen paired stratified row bootstrap with 2,000 replicates and seed 42. Stage27 used its own 2,000-replicate stratified row bootstrap. Stage25 used deterministic equations, not resampling. Stage28 reported finite training-seed variation separately from bootstrap intervals. No bootstrap procedure is treated as universal across stages, and a confidence interval containing zero does not prove equivalence.

Anti-adaptation controls were protocol-level: development-only preprocessing and tuning; validation-only threshold selection; source-only transfer; known-family validation thresholds in LOAO; locked feature and semantic bridges; one-time historical target openings; and permanent closure of consumed targets. No target was used for fitting, feature search, calibration, threshold reselection, or model choice. Cancelled `GROUNDED_S4` cells remain cancelled. These controls restrict interpretation to the declared populations and do not create independence guarantees absent from the data.

## 4.10 Reproducibility and Provenance

The evidence chain links each claim to a frozen result, table, or figure; its stage protocol; a canonical notebook, script, or extracted implementation; the original notebook cells; and provenance or equivalence records. The repository distinguishes raw-exact, published, corrected, source-faithful, and reconstructed artifacts.
