# 5. Results

## 5.1 Conventional Benchmark Performance

The conventional question was within-table discrimination on the frozen, processed CSE-CIC-IDS2018 reference population. The validation-selected balanced operating point was XGBoost at threshold 0.51; the security-constrained point was LightGBM at threshold 0.26. On the untouched reference holdout, balanced XGBoost achieved F1 0.9285, FPR 0.0061, ROC-AUC 0.9802, and PR-AUC 0.9776. The security LightGBM point achieved F1 0.9178, F2 0.9112, FPR 0.0466, ROC-AUC 0.9802, and PR-AUC 0.9777 (Table 1; E29-004–E29-007; NUM29-014–NUM29-029).

These results establish strong discrimination under the processed and stratified reference condition, not temporal, cross-dataset, prevalence, deployment, or unseen-family capability. The balanced XGBoost-minus-LightGBM F1 difference was 0.000185, with a historical paired class-stratified 95% interval of [-0.000495, 0.000862], which did not resolve a balanced learner difference (E29-009; NUM29-034–NUM29-036). At the security operating points, the LightGBM-favored recall difference was -0.001364 for XGBoost minus LightGBM, with interval [-0.002646, -0.000083]; the corresponding false-negative difference was 33, with interval [2, 64] (E29-010; NUM29-037–NUM29-042). This result is conditional on the frozen holdout and does not establish universal LightGBM superiority.

Calibration likewise did not yield a resolved learner winner. Brier scores were 0.042774 for XGBoost and 0.042754 for LightGBM; all paired calibration-difference intervals included zero (E29-011; NUM29-043–NUM29-044). The known-category audit remains separate: processed-reference `Infilteration` support was 3,967, not the 36-positive LOAO `Infiltration` population used later (E29-013; NUM29-049). Full uncertainty, calibration, and support details remain in Supplement S1 and Tables S1–S3.

## 5.2 Temporal Validity

The temporal question was whether development geometry changed ranking and frozen operating-point transfer on the same K79-clean Mar1–Mar2 forward target. The target contained 1,374,133 rows: 375,345 attacks and 998,788 benign flows, with attack prevalence 0.273150 (E29-020; NUM29-078–NUM29-081). The random-natural and chronological-natural cells used the same target, while their development memberships differed. This distinction is essential: the development validation populations were not the final forward population.

On the single seed-42 opening, random-natural produced final PR-AUC 0.262953 and ROC-AUC 0.520490. Chronological-natural produced PR-AUC 0.610734 and ROC-AUC 0.806418; chronological-rebalanced produced PR-AUC 0.692630 and ROC-AUC 0.832164 (E29-020; NUM29-082–NUM29-087). The result was not a post-holdout winner selection. It shows a direction-specific contrast under the frozen development geometries. Chronology and attack-family composition changed together, so these results do not identify chronology as a sole causal factor.

Figure 1 preserves PR-AUC and ROC-AUC as separate native panels. Stage28 repeated the designated random-natural and chronological-natural cells across five frozen seeds on the historically opened shared target. Random-natural mean ROC-AUC was 0.5176 (SD 0.0073) and mean PR-AUC was 0.2599 (SD 0.0034); chronological-natural means were 0.8209 (SD 0.0085) and 0.6388 (SD 0.0322), respectively. The chronological value exceeded the random value in all five realizations for both metrics (E29-037–E29-038; NUM29-135–NUM29-144; Table 2). This 5/5 ordering is descriptive over the registered seeds, not a population-level guarantee or significance test.

Ranking transfer did not imply operating-point transfer. The chronological balanced/security thresholds almost never fired on the forward target, whereas the two random security points produced final FPR values 0.2870 and 0.2685, far above the development-era 5% constraint (E29-021; NUM29-088–NUM29-089). The forward target was historically opened once and later reused under the frozen stability protocol; it is not presented as a new blind replication. K79 establishes exact corrected-second-plus-predictor identity control only, not S4, session, endpoint, source-IP, or five-tuple independence.

## 5.3 Shortcut and Explanation Validity

The shortcut question was whether feature-subset effects were invariant across development geometry and learner, and whether explanation stability implied local fidelity. Stage23 evaluated seven frozen subsets on the random-natural and chronological populations. In the full 70-feature condition, random and chronological PR-AUC were 0.995590 and 0.106215, respectively. Removing `Dst Port` produced PR-AUC 0.995260 and 0.102474; the registered `NO_PORTS` condition produced 0.995267 and 0.102473. The behavior-only subset produced 0.981588 under random development and 0.095540 under chronological development (E29-022; `table_23_1_primary_subset_performance.csv`; Supplement S2).

The subset effects were split- and learner-dependent. For example, the `NO_PORTS` PR-AUC removal penalty was 0.000323 in the random condition and 0.003742 in the chronological condition, with a split interaction of -0.003419 and a conditional interval [-0.005286, -0.001548]. The behavior-only condition had removal penalties 0.014002 and 0.010675, with an interaction interval that included zero for PR-AUC. ROC-AUC interactions were likewise not uniform (E29-022–E29-023; T29-S07). Matched-size placebos, stump probes, proxy-absorption checks, and family-support records constrained interpretation but did not identify a single causal shortcut mechanism. `NO_PORTS` removed `Dst Port` and `Protocol`; `Src Port` was absent and is not treated as removed.

TreeSHAP produced partial agreement between the two tree learners on a 5,000-row shared background: 15 of the top 20 features overlapped, Jaccard similarity was 0.60, and Spearman rank agreement was 0.854066 (E29-008; NUM29-030–NUM29-033). These are descriptive attribution comparisons. In the 64-case deterministic LIME panel, only 2 cases met all criteria, 31 failed local decision reproduction, and mean top-10 SHAP–LIME Jaccard agreement was 0.304 for XGBoost and 0.360 for LightGBM (E29-015; NUM29-055–NUM29-059). Thus, perturbation stability did not establish local fidelity. Integrated Gradients and Stage17 attention diagnostics remain restricted to their documented neural-model contexts and are not treated as evidence for a common architecture explanation.

The approved main exhibit is Figure 2. Full subset, placebo, stump, proxy, family-support, LIME, TreeSHAP, Integrated Gradients, and attention evidence remains supplementary or archival under S1–S2 and Tables S2–S3. These observations establish conditional representation and explanation sensitivity, not leakage, causality, or a universal transfer mechanism.

## 5.4 Cross-Dataset Transfer

Stage24 asked whether ranking transferred in both source-to-target directions under frozen semantic bridges. The IDS2018-to-CICIDS2017 target contained 2,830,743 effective flows, including 557,646 attacks and prevalence 0.196996. Under bridge62, PR-AUC was 0.667483 and ROC-AUC was 0.733946. The reciprocal CICIDS2017-to-IDS2018 evaluation used the frozen February 28 target with prevalence 0.104847; bridge62 achieved PR-AUC 0.108176 and ROC-AUC 0.525167 (E29-024–E29-025; NUM29-090–NUM29-095; Table 3).

The directions are not averaged. The forward bridge retained ranking signal, whereas reciprocal PR-AUC was close to its target-prevalence anchor and ROC-AUC was near 0.5. The source learners, target populations, preprocessing, and prevalence structures differ by direction; the result therefore supports directional asymmetry only for the evaluated contracts.

Bridge62 published and flag-corrected representations were bitwise identical because bridge62 excludes all eight aggregate flag-count fields. Bridge70 retained published and corrected serialization as distinct conditions. In the IDS2018-to-CICIDS2017 comparison, bridge70 correction changed PR-AUC by -0.007729, ROC-AUC by +0.002192, and Brier score by +0.005087; the registered paired intervals excluded zero. The metric directions differed, so the result is not a single degradation score (E29-026; NUM29-096–NUM29-098; Supplement S3, Table S5).

The two planned `GROUNDED_S4` cells were cancelled before target opening because durable physical-row membership could not be recovered without a new heuristic. They have no empirical measurements and are not interpreted as negative model results (E29-027; NUM29-099). Figure 3 and Table 3 retain direction-specific bridge62 metrics; bridge70 sensitivity, threshold transfer, and cancellation governance remain in Supplement S3 and Tables S4–S5.

## 5.5 Prevalence and Operational Consequences

Stage25 translated frozen operating points under an analytic prior-probability-shift model. It did not refit models, reopen targets, or retune thresholds. At assumed attack prevalence 0.1%, with one million benign flows per day and two minutes per alert, the random-natural STANDARD point projected PPV 0.965572 and 33.5 analyst-hours per day. The projected burden included approximately 34.6 false alerts and 969.4 true alerts per day (E29-028; NUM29-100–NUM29-102; Table 4).

The chronological STANDARD point showed the opposite counterexample: projected PPV was 0.000551068, with 0.0322 true alerts per day and approximately 1.9 analyst-hours per day under the same scenario (E29-029; NUM29-103–NUM29-104). Low workload therefore reflected almost no detected attacks, not validated operational usefulness. For the forward transfer STANDARD points, projected PPV ranged from 0.039233 to 0.060313; for reverse transfer, it ranged from 0.000257610 to 0.000287993 (E29-030; NUM29-105–NUM29-108). Under the declared relative cost setting C_FP:C_FN = 1:100, 15 of 24 points favored the model at 0.1% prevalence, compared with 3 of 24 at 0.01% (E29-031; NUM29-109–NUM29-110).

Figure 4 shows the PPV cliff under fixed TPR/FPR assumptions. Table 4 reports selected 0.1% translations. These values are deterministic projections conditional on inherited operating rates, one-million-flow traffic, two-minute service time, analyst-capacity scenarios, and relative costs; they are not observations from a real SOC. The projections do not account for simultaneous covariate, protocol, topology, user, attacker, or extractor shift.

## 5.6 Deployment Characteristics

Stage26 asked which compatible prepared-input-to-probability paths were computationally feasible under the recorded hardware and software conditions. At batch size one, p95 CPU/GPU ratios were 1.94 for the five-checkpoint soft-voting ensemble and 1.58 for the single-resource reference. CatBoost and XGBoost ratios were 0.16 and 0.26, indicating CPU advantage under the registered ratio convention. The masked CNN ratio was 10.86 and the masked ViT ratio was 1.05 (E29-032; NUM29-111–NUM29-116; Figure 5).

The ratios are component-level point estimates, not confidence intervals or end-to-end detection latencies. They describe compatible paths on the recorded CPU/Tesla-T4 environments and frozen batch conditions. Unsupported LightGBM GPU execution, resource-limited paths, timeouts, and unavailable backends remain status values rather than imputed timings. Packet capture, flow extraction, representation construction, alert aggregation, transport, and analyst response were outside the timed boundary. Full warm/cold, memory, package, component, matched-resource, backend, and Pareto details remain in Supplement S5 and Tables S7–S10 as routed by the Stage29 registry.

Stage15 FT-Transformer, Stage16 classical paths, Stage18 representation feasibility, Stage19 temporal models, Stage20 packet-image CNN, and Stage21 CNN–ViT comparison provide historical architecture chronology and compatibility context. Stage18 audited seven tabular ViT representations with zero passing the feasibility gate; Stage21's ViT-over-CNN Friday comparison remains descriptive and supplement-only. These records do not establish architecture superiority or universal latency behavior.

## 5.7 Unseen-Family Evaluation

The LOAO question was whether a held-out family could be ranked against temporally matched benign traffic with zero exposure in training and validation. Five of seven taxonomy families were eligible: BOT, DDOS, INFILTRATION, PORT_SCAN, and WEB_ATTACK. DOS and AUTH_BRUTE_FORCE were structurally ineligible. `Infiltration` was executable but descriptive because its held-out target contained 36 positives (E29-034–E29-035; NUM29-117–NUM29-118; Table 5).

Transfer was selective. For DDoS, XGBoost achieved ROC-AUC 0.9982 and PR-AUC 0.9925; LightGBM achieved ROC-AUC 0.9986 and PR-AUC 0.9940. For Web Attack, the corresponding values were 0.9693/0.7206 and 0.9901/0.7605. Bot was learner-dependent: ROC-AUC was 0.3224 for XGBoost and 0.5591 for LightGBM, with XGBoost PR-AUC 0.003256. Port Scan ROC-AUC was 0.5506 for XGBoost and 0.7559 for LightGBM (E29-034; NUM29-119–NUM29-132).

Ranking did not guarantee frozen-threshold recall. At the balanced operating point, Bot recall was 0.00% for both learners; DDoS recall was 66.20% for XGBoost and 26.25% for LightGBM; Port Scan recall was 0.48% and 1.17%; and Web Attack recall was 77.80% and 86.10%, respectively. Infiltration balanced recall was 0.00% for both learners and remains descriptive. These threshold results are distinct from ROC-AUC and PR-AUC and were obtained with known-family validation thresholds only.

Figure 6 and Table 5 preserve family- and learner-specific outcomes. Stage28 stability classified DDoS, Port Scan, and Web Attack qualitative conditions as stable across both learners, while Bot remained learner-dependent and Infiltration descriptive (E29-039). The result is selective eligible-family transfer, not universal zero-day detection. Full primary metrics, intervals, support, and seed-level details are routed to Supplement S6 and Tables S8–S9.

## 5.8 Seed and Control Stability

Stage28 completed 108/108 authorized new fits, reused 12 declared artifacts, and left zero planned cells outstanding (E29-041; NUM29-132–NUM29-134). Completion documents closure of the registered plan; it does not broaden the estimand. No best seed was selected, full historical hyperparameter search was repeated, or new significance test was introduced.

The temporal ranking direction survived all five frozen seed linkages: chronological-natural exceeded random-natural for both PR-AUC and ROC-AUC in 5/5 comparisons (E29-038; NUM29-143–NUM29-144). This result strengthens the fixed-recipe temporal contrast within the shared historical target. It does not establish a population guarantee, a new blind holdout, or temporal causation. Family-level stability was heterogeneous: DDoS and Web Attack retained strong qualitative conditions across learners, Port Scan retained its qualitative condition but remained learner-dependent in magnitude, Bot remained learner-dependent, and Infiltration remained descriptive (E29-039; Table 5).

The matched random-LOAO controls were mixed and family-specific. They did not isolate chronology, family composition, novelty difficulty, or a single drift mechanism as the unique explanation for the temporal contrast (E29-040). The controls therefore qualify mechanism claims rather than produce a causal attribution.

## 5.9 Integrated Validity Assessment

Table 6 synthesizes the nine native-metric axes without normalizing them into a robustness score. Several observations survived stricter evaluation: strong within-reference discrimination remained true for the processed reference; chronological development retained higher forward ranking across the five frozen seed realizations; the forward bridge62 direction retained cross-dataset ranking signal; several eligible families remained discriminable; and compatible inference paths were measurable on the recorded hardware.

Other findings changed or remained conditional. Frozen operating thresholds transferred poorly despite improved forward ranking. Reverse transfer was near its prevalence and ROC-AUC anchors. Shortcut effects varied by split and learner, and placebo/control interactions prevented a causal leakage claim. High projected PPV did not guarantee manageable workload, while low workload could reflect negligible yield. Computational feasibility applied only to measured compatible paths. LOAO outcomes depended on family, learner, threshold, and support; DOS and AUTH_BRUTE_FORCE were not executable, and Infiltration remained descriptive at 36 positives.

The intended `GROUNDED_S4` experiments remained cancelled, unsupported runtime paths remained statuses, and Stage18 tabular ViT feasibility did not produce a performance result. Stage18 graph and Stage21 CNN–ViT results remain descriptive/supplement-only. Accordingly, the evidence does not justify a universal generalization claim, a symmetric portability claim, a universal zero-day claim, an end-to-end deployment-latency claim, or a single causal explanation for the temporal contrast. Table 6 is a synthesis of frozen results and interpretation ceilings, not an additional experiment.
