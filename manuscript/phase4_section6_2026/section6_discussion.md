# 6. Discussion

## 6.1 Benchmark Performance Is Not Deployment Evidence

The conventional reference evaluation answered a deliberately narrow question: whether selected learners could discriminate attack from benign traffic within a frozen, processed, stratified CSE-CIC-IDS2018 population. It provided a credible reference condition. Strong holdout F1, ROC-AUC, and PR-AUC therefore remain meaningful evidence of within-reference discrimination, rather than evidence that should be dismissed because later audits exposed additional validity boundaries. The unresolved balanced XGBoost–LightGBM contrast and the absence of a resolved calibration winner further indicate that the reference condition did not identify a universal learner or probability-quality advantage.

The inferential boundary is central. The reference population was processed and stratified, used a frozen membership structure, and did not reproduce natural temporal ordering or deployment prevalence. Later results showed why those distinctions matter: forward ranking depended on development geometry, cross-dataset behavior was directional, prevalence projections separated precision from workload, and LOAO performance varied by family and learner. The benchmark is thus best interpreted as a controlled baseline against which stricter questions can be posed. It neither validates the deployment of the evaluated models nor becomes irrelevant to the multi-axis audit.

## 6.2 Temporal Validity and Operating-Point Transfer

The temporal audit changed the interpretation of conventional ranking without reducing the result to a simple chronological-versus-random winner. On the shared forward target, chronological development produced higher ranking than random development under the frozen protocol, and the ordering persisted across all five registered Stage28 seed linkages. This is evidence that development geometry materially changes the estimate obtained by a benchmark evaluation. It is not evidence that chronological splitting is universally superior, because chronology was accompanied by changes in attack-family composition and the target was a historically opened shared holdout rather than a new prospective field sample.

Ranking and operating-point utility separated sharply. The chronological cells ranked the forward target more effectively, yet their frozen thresholds almost never fired, whereas the random security points produced forward false-positive rates far above the development-era constraint. This distinction has practical and methodological importance: a model can preserve ordering while failing to transport a decision rule. Threshold selection, calibration, prevalence, and action costs therefore require evaluation separately from threshold-free ranking. The five-seed result strengthens the bounded fixed-recipe observation, but its 5/5 ordering is descriptive rather than a significance test or population guarantee. K79 also establishes exact corrected-second-plus-predictor identity separation only; it does not establish session, endpoint, source-IP, five-tuple, or S4 independence.

## 6.3 Shortcut Features and Explainability Reliability

The shortcut audit narrows, rather than completes, the explanation of the temporal contrast. Feature-subset effects varied with split and learner. The `NO_PORTS` interaction differed between random and chronological conditions, while matched-size placebos and family-support diagnostics showed that similar interaction behavior could arise under control constructions. These observations support sensitivity to representation and development geometry. They do not identify a single shortcut, prove leakage, or establish that one feature family caused cross-dataset failure. Feature removal is an intervention on the tested representation, not a causal intervention on the data-generating process.

The explanation results impose a related qualification. TreeSHAP rankings showed partial agreement between the two tree learners, which is useful descriptive evidence of shared attribution structure under the declared background sample. LIME, however, frequently failed the study's local decision-reproduction criteria despite perturbation-based comparisons. Attribution agreement, perturbation repeatability, and local fidelity are therefore distinct properties. Integrated Gradients and attention diagnostics remain bounded to their documented neural-model contexts; they cannot be generalized into a common explanation for the tree, temporal, graph, CNN, and ViT branches. The combined evidence supports explanation auditing as a validity check, not attribution as causal proof.

## 6.4 Directional Cross-Dataset Generalization

The transfer results show why portability should be treated as a directional property of a source model, target population, preprocessing chain, and feature contract. IDS2018-to-CICIDS2017 retained ranking signal under bridge62, whereas CICIDS2017-to-IDS2018 was close to the target prevalence and ROC-AUC anchors. These outcomes cannot be averaged into a symmetric transfer score. The directions used different source and target roles, prevalence structures, and validation histories, so the asymmetry is a property of the evaluated contracts rather than a universal ordering of the datasets.

Bridge62 and bridge70 also answer different questions. Bridge62 excludes the eight aggregate flag-count fields, making its published and corrected representations bitwise identical. Bridge70 retains those fields and showed metric-specific sensitivity to corrected serialization: PR-AUC, ROC-AUC, and Brier changed in different directions. This is evidence that feature semantics and serialization can alter transfer measurements, not evidence for one common degradation mechanism. The two `GROUNDED_S4` cells remain cancelled because durable physical-row membership could not be recovered without a new heuristic. Their absence marks an incomplete planned sensitivity analysis; it is not a model failure and supplies no empirical negative result.

## 6.5 Ranking, Precision, Workload, and Operational Utility

The prevalence analysis translates frozen rates into decision-relevant quantities, but it does not observe a Security Operations Center. Under the declared fixed-TPR/FPR prior-shift model, one million benign flows per day, two minutes of analyst service per alert, capacity assumptions, and relative costs, the random-natural standard point retained high projected PPV while still generating a large analyst-hour burden. Conversely, the chronological standard point generated little workload because it detected almost no attacks under the same projection. These paired examples show why PPV, alert volume, analyst time, and detection yield must be considered together.

The same logic applies to transfer. Forward and reverse transfer points produced substantially different projected PPV under the 0.1% scenario, while the relative-cost decisions varied with prevalence. These are deterministic projections conditional on inherited operating rates and declared assumptions. They do not account for simultaneous covariate, protocol, topology, user, attacker, or extractor shift, and they do not establish field workload, organizational economics, or production utility. Operational evaluation should therefore report the assumptions and yield alongside precision rather than treating one favorable quantity as sufficient.

## 6.6 Computational and Representation Boundaries

Stage26 contributes feasibility evidence at a defined boundary: compatible prepared-input-to-materialized-probability paths on the recorded CPU/Tesla-T4 software environments and batch conditions. Its p95 comparisons demonstrate that CPU/GPU behavior depends on model family, implementation, and batch size. A ratio below one for some classical learners is not a universal statement about CPU superiority, just as a measured GPU advantage for another path is not a portable deployment constant.

The measured boundary excludes packet capture, flow construction, feature extraction, representation conversion, alert aggregation, transport, and analyst response. Unsupported backends, timeouts, and resource-limited paths remain status values and cannot be converted into poor timings. Earlier FT-Transformer, graph, MTemporal, packet-image CNN, ViT, and explanation experiments used separate populations, contracts, and environments. Stage18's rejected tabular ViT feasibility branch is not a performance result; Stage18 graph and Stage21 CNN–ViT comparisons remain descriptive or supplement-only. Consequently, the study informs compatibility and measurement design, not universal architecture ranking, energy efficiency, or end-to-end IDS latency.

## 6.7 Unseen Attack-Family Generalization

The LOAO audit provides a more demanding test than known-category evaluation because the held-out family was excluded from training and validation under the registered chronology. Its conclusion is selective transfer. DDoS and Web Attack retained strong ranking for both learners, whereas Bot was learner-dependent and Port Scan showed materially different learner rankings. The threshold results were not interchangeable with ranking: some families with useful ROC-AUC or PR-AUC had weak frozen-threshold recall, while the operating behavior differed by learner.

The eligibility rules define the scope. DOS and AUTH_BRUTE_FORCE were structurally ineligible, and Infiltration had only 36 held-out positives, so it remains descriptive. This support must not be conflated with the known-category `Infilteration` support of 3,967 in the processed reference audit. The LOAO results therefore support family-specific behavior under an eligible benchmark protocol, not universal zero-day detection or an aggregate novelty capability. They also show why withholding a label family is not sufficient by itself: support, chronology, learner, threshold, and target construction all affect the interpretation.

## 6.8 Stability, Controls, and Mechanistic Uncertainty

Stage28 closed the registered empirical plan with 108 of 108 new fits, 12 declared artifact reuses, and no remaining planned cells. This closure strengthens governance and makes the registered stability statements auditable; it does not enlarge the estimand. The temporal ordering survived five frozen seed realizations, while family-level conditions remained heterogeneous. Seed variation is a different uncertainty object from the stage-specific bootstrap intervals and should not be combined into an unregistered significance claim.

The matched random-LOAO controls were mixed and family-specific. They therefore constrain explanations of the temporal contrast without isolating chronology, family composition, novelty difficulty, or one form of concept drift as its unique cause. Failure to isolate a mechanism does not show that no mechanism exists; it shows that the completed controls do not identify one. This distinction is important for a multi-axis audit: stronger repeatability of a bounded observation need not imply a stronger causal or external-validity claim.

## 6.9 Reproducibility and Evidence Boundaries

The completed work has a structured but heterogeneous reproducibility profile. Protocol configurations, safe interfaces, archived notebooks, source maps, result manifests, hashes, execution receipts, and claim-to-artifact registries preserve the provenance chain. Stage25 supports deterministic scalar reproduction from frozen inputs. Stage26 preserves the profiling methodology and archival hardware measurements, although its timings remain environment-specific. Many earlier results are static or partially reproducible because source datasets, closed targets, historical models, probability or explanation arrays, and exact software environments are unavailable or intentionally not reopened.

This distinction prevents two opposite errors. A documented notebook or configuration is not equivalent to a complete local rerun, while a closed target or hash-only artifact is not evidence that a value was invented. The manuscript consequently treats claim-level verification, static artifact inspection, and deterministic calculation as different evidence classes. Stage29 added synthesis and traceability; it did not add empirical computation. The frozen-holdout restrictions remain in force, including the prohibition on reopening targets for manuscript preparation.

## 6.10 Implications for IDS Evaluation Practice

The study supports an evidence-informed evaluation sequence rather than a universally validated new standard. First, define the population, provenance, prevalence, duplicate policy, feature contract, and split before interpreting performance. A benchmark metric is only as portable as the population and decision rule that produced it.

Second, test forward temporal behavior on a declared target, while reporting ranking and operating-point transfer separately. Third, probe feature sensitivity with matched controls and treat explanation outputs as evidence about attribution behavior and fidelity, not as causal proof. Fourth, evaluate cross-dataset transfer in both directions where feasible, preserving source and target roles, semantic bridges, preprocessing, and serialization conditions.

Fifth, translate frozen rates under explicit base-rate, traffic-volume, service-time, capacity, and cost assumptions. Sixth, profile the declared computational boundary, retaining hardware, software, batch, component, and unsupported-path qualifications. Seventh, gate withheld-family analyses by chronology, zero exposure, eligibility, and positive support; do not convert ineligible or low-support folds into universal novelty claims. Finally, repeat critical observations across registered model realizations and distinguish finite-seed stability from statistical significance.

Taken together, these practices encourage claims to follow the strongest evidence actually available. The audit does not establish that every axis must be implemented identically in every IDS study, nor that passing one axis compensates for failure on another. Its practical implication is narrower: benchmark performance, temporal validity, transfer, operational translation, computational feasibility, and unseen-family behavior should be reported as related but non-interchangeable questions.
