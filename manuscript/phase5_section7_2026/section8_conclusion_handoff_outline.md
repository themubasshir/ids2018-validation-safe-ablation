Section 8 is not drafted here. The conclusion should synthesize only the following evidence-bounded findings.

## Defensible findings

1. The processed CSE-CIC-IDS2018 reference produced strong within-reference discrimination under frozen validation-selected operating points.
2. Chronological development produced higher forward ranking than random development across five registered fixed-recipe realizations on the shared historical target.
3. Ranking transfer and frozen operating-point transfer diverged; improved ranking did not guarantee useful threshold behavior.
4. Shortcut effects were split- and learner-dependent; explanation agreement and local fidelity were not interchangeable.
5. Cross-dataset transfer was directional under the evaluated bridge contracts; bridge semantics and serialization affected measurements.
6. Prevalence translation showed that PPV, alert volume, workload, yield, and cost preference are distinct quantities.
7. Computational feasibility was established only for measured compatible paths at the recorded hardware/software/component boundary.
8. LOAO behavior was selective and learner/family/support-dependent; several eligible families retained ranking, while some outcomes were weak or descriptive.
9. Evidence traceability and reproducibility governance were substantial but heterogeneous; only Stage25 supports full deterministic scalar reproduction from frozen inputs.

## Prohibited conclusion claims

- The model generalizes without qualification.
- Universal zero-day detection.
- Chronology universally improves IDS.
- Shortcut features cause transfer failure.
- A causal explanation for the temporal contrast.
- Symmetric or bridge-independent cross-dataset portability.
- Real SOC deployment utility or end-to-end latency.
- Universal CNN, ViT, or learner superiority.
- A composite robustness/generalization score.
- Complete local reproduction of all historical science.
