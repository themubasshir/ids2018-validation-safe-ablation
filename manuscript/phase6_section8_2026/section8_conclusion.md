# 8. Conclusion

This study investigated whether intrusion-detection conclusions that appear strong under conventional benchmark evaluation remain informative when assessment is extended across temporal ordering, feature sensitivity, cross-dataset transfer, operational assumptions, computational conditions, and withheld attack families. Its contribution is an evidence-grounded, multi-axis audit of IDS validity rather than a new detection architecture or a claim of universal model superiority. The resulting framework preserves the value of a controlled benchmark while making the population, operating rule, realization, and evidence boundary explicit.

The processed and stratified CSE-CIC-IDS2018 reference produced strong discrimination for the selected tree ensembles under its frozen evaluation condition. That result did not determine a universal learner or calibration winner. Under the evaluated temporal protocol, chronological development produced higher forward ranking than random development across five registered fixed-recipe realizations, while the inherited operating thresholds transferred poorly to the shared forward target. Feature sensitivities and ablation effects varied by split and learner; repeatable attribution did not guarantee local explanation fidelity, and the controls did not identify one causal shortcut mechanism. Cross-dataset transfer was directional under the frozen IDS2018/CICIDS2017 bridge, with the result dependent on bridge semantics, preprocessing, and transfer direction.

The operational analyses further showed why discrimination metrics cannot stand alone. Under fixed-rate prevalence scenarios, predictive value, alert volume, analyst workload, detection yield, and cost preference could diverge. These projections were not field SOC measurements. Similarly, computational feasibility was demonstrated only for supported paths on the recorded hardware and software stack, not as end-to-end IDS latency or hardware-independent superiority. Withheld-family behavior was selective and dependent on family, learner, metric, and threshold. DOS and AUTH_BRUTE_FORCE were ineligible under the registered LOAO chronology, while the 36-positive Infiltration fold remained descriptive; neither supports a universal zero-day claim.

The conclusions remain conditional on two benchmark families, frozen target and bridge contracts, finite registered realizations, and heterogeneous artifact availability. Chronology and family composition were not fully separated, Stage28 reused the historically opened target, and K79 controlled exact available-record identity rather than session, endpoint, source-IP, five-tuple, or S4 independence. The repository supports substantial provenance and selected deterministic checks, but not complete end-to-end regeneration of every historical result.

Future work should test these boundaries with independent prospective targets, broader source populations and attack-family coverage, explicit session- and infrastructure-level independence controls, reproducible execution environments, and measured operational pipelines. The central conclusion is methodological: IDS evidence becomes more interpretable when each claim states the population, metric, operating rule, realization, and evidence status under which it holds. Multi-axis validity evaluation therefore narrows overclaiming without discarding the legitimate information provided by conventional benchmark performance.
