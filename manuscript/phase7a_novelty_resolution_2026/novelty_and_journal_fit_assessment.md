# Novelty and journal-fit assessment

## Primary recommendation

**Position as a bounded source-faithful empirical IDS2018/CICIDS2017 validity audit, with an evidence-governed interpretation layer.** This is more defensible than “integrated empirical framework,” “protocol stress test” alone, or “reproducibility study” alone.

The paper's value is the completed empirical pattern under explicit contracts: strong processed-reference discrimination; chronological-over-random forward ranking under five frozen linkages; poor frozen-threshold transfer; split/learner-dependent feature sensitivity; directional bridge transfer; prevalence/workload divergence; hardware-specific feasibility; selective eligible-family behavior; and explicit evidence ceilings. The pattern is heterogeneous, not compressible into one robustness score.

## JISA fit

**Conditional fit: plausible, not secured.** JISA can accommodate empirical IDS evaluation and deployment-oriented validity analysis. Fit depends on presenting a substantive bounded result, not a checklist expansion. The manuscript must show why the linked audit changes scientific interpretation for the stated datasets and protocols, while candidly acknowledging Berhili, Elangovan, Mishra, Ha Thanh, Biswas, and Buriboyev overlap. Biswas materially reduces any defensible claim that the temporal or attack-family questions themselves are conceptually novel.

## Likely reviewer objections and response requirements

1. **“Berhili or Biswas already did this.”** Acknowledge direct overlap early. State differences as empirical scope, frozen contracts, source-faithful chronology, operating-point transfer, eligible LOAO, workload projections, measured paths, and evidence-state handling—not as invention of multi-axis evaluation. Biswas specifically establishes temporal-protocol sensitivity on CSE-CIC-IDS2018 and weakens any temporal/attack-family priority claim.
2. **“This is only a longer checklist.”** Lead with the heterogeneous findings and their non-equivalence. Explain which conclusions survive, fail, or remain unresolved.
3. **“Different datasets do not establish novelty.”** Agree. Use dataset differences only to bound empirical comparability; novelty language must rest on protocol-specific observations and evidence governance.
4. **“Temporal result proves concept drift.”** Do not claim this. Chronology and family composition are entangled; controls did not isolate one mechanism.
5. **“LOAO proves zero-day detection.”** Do not claim this. Retain eligibility, support, learner, and taxonomy limits; DOS/AUTH_BRUTE_FORCE are ineligible; Infiltration support is 36.
6. **“Operational results are deployment validation.”** Call them fixed-rate projections under stated analyst-time/cost assumptions, not field SOC outcomes.
7. **“Hardware results establish latency.”** Limit to measured compatible paths, recorded hardware/software, and component-level profiling; unsupported backends remain unsupported.
8. **“Reproducibility package equals independent reproduction.”** Distinguish available artifacts, static verification, and executed rerun.
9. **“Why not a factorial causal audit?”** State that the study reports registered empirical contrasts and controls; cancellation and mixed controls constrain causal interpretation.
10. **“Why JISA rather than a methods venue?”** Emphasize security-application implications: threshold behavior, workload, family selectivity, and deployment boundaries, not only protocol mechanics.

## Additional conflict and stop condition

Biswas (2026) is a further material overlap: it evaluates CSE-CIC-IDS2018 under random, stratified-temporal, and class-blind chronological protocols, and reports unseen-family, cross-dataset, and calibration-related interpretation. This directly establishes the temporal-protocol problem and weakens any conceptual novelty claim for temporal or attack-family evaluation. The remaining distinction is bounded empirical scope and contract-specific observations, not method invention.

**Controlled checkpoint stop:** because this comparator materially changes the novelty assessment, do not commit or push until the author reviews the added comparator and confirms the narrowed positioning. Existing evidence does not require abandoning the bounded case-study framing, but it prevents treating A as an independently novel temporal contribution.

## Readiness decision

**Phase 7B: HOLD pending author/reviewer decision on narrowed positioning.** Writing can proceed only after the overlap report, Biswas conflict, and contribution merge/narrow decision are accepted. If the authors require a strong methodological novelty claim, current evidence is insufficient; no new experiments should be invented in prose. A credible JISA submission remains possible as a bounded empirical case study, but acceptance cannot be predicted from the present comparison.
