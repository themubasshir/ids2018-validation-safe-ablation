# 1. Introduction

## 1.1 Research motivation

Machine-learning intrusion-detection benchmarks provide useful controlled evidence. A fixed population, representation, learner, and decision rule can support reproducible comparison of discrimination and operating behavior. Such evidence should not be discarded because it is conditional. It answers a narrower question: how well does a fitted detector separate the labels in the evaluated benchmark under the declared protocol? Network intrusion detection, however, is applied to traffic whose chronology, collection environment, feature contract, prevalence, attack composition, and computational path may differ from those conditions [@sommer2010closed; @ring2019survey; @arp2022dos].

Consequently, strong benchmark discrimination does not by itself establish future-facing reliability. Ranking metrics describe score ordering, whereas a deployed decision rule depends on a threshold, calibration, prevalence, and action cost [@saito2015precision; @axelsson2000baserate]. Cross-dataset evidence additionally depends on source-to-target direction, semantic feature correspondence, preprocessing, and target construction [@dhooge2020interdataset; @layeghy2023crossdomain]. Feature ablations and explanations can reveal sensitivity, but neither feature removal nor attribution repeatability alone identifies a causal shortcut or faithful local reasoning [@engelen2021troubleshooting; @arp2022dos; @ribeiro2016lime; @lundberg2017shap; @yeh2019infidelity]. Operational interpretation requires alert volume, workload, yield, and assumptions about base rates; computational claims require a declared hardware and software boundary. Finally, withholding an attack family is more demanding than evaluating known categories, but its interpretation remains dependent on eligibility, support, learner, metric, and threshold.

These quantities should remain separate. A model may rank a forward target better while its inherited threshold transfers poorly. A direction may retain cross-dataset ranking while the reciprocal direction is near its prevalence and chance anchors. High projected positive predictive value may coexist with a burdensome alert stream, while low workload may reflect negligible detection yield. Such tensions are not one-dimensional robustness failures; they define the conditions under which each inference is informative.

## 1.2 Existing knowledge

Prior research has already established important parts of this evaluation problem. Dataset audits document extraction errors, artifacts, and dependence concerns in widely used intrusion-detection benchmarks [@engelen2021troubleshooting; @arp2022dos]. Time-aware evaluation provides a future-use direction, while work on experimental bias shows that split geometry and collection order can change the interpretation of machine-learning results [@pendlebury2019tesseract]. Cross-dataset studies examine transfer strength, shared representations, and domain dependence [@dhooge2020interdataset; @layeghy2023crossdomain; @cantone2024crossdataset]. Explainability research provides tools such as LIME, SHAP, and TreeSHAP, together with separate concepts of sensitivity and fidelity [@ribeiro2016lime; @lundberg2017shap; @lundberg2020trees; @yeh2019infidelity]. Base-rate analysis and prior-probability adjustment explain why discrimination and positive predictive value cannot be interpreted independently [@axelsson2000baserate; @saerens2002prior]. Open-set and unknown-attack studies likewise provide established protocols for testing behavior beyond development classes [@scheirer2013openset; @zoppi2023unknown; @shin2023openset].

Recent work makes the overlap with the present study especially direct. Berhili, Chaieb, and Benabdellah investigate protocol-dependent inflation across three datasets, with documented analyses of validation-split bias, seed sensitivity, preprocessing leakage, semantically aligned transfer, calibration, threshold choices, statistical testing, and reproducibility artifacts [@berhili2026protocol; @berhili2026reproducibility]. Their article-level access was incomplete in the present review, so uninspected dimensions are not treated as omissions. Biswas reports a non-peer-reviewed preprint using CSE-CIC-IDS2018 and CIC-DDoS2019 to compare random, stratified-temporal, and class-blind chronological protocols, cross-regime transfer, calibration, and unseen-family interpretation [@biswas2026drift]. This work directly establishes the relevance of temporal-protocol sensitivity on IDS2018; the present study therefore makes no conceptual-priority claim for temporal validation or unseen-family testing.

Elangovan et al. study temporal and semantic cross-dataset generalization across four public datasets [@elangovan2026crossdataset]. Mishra et al. report harmonized cross-dataset multi-model learning with statistical validation [@mishra2026harmonized]; their associated correction concerns the funding statement, not the methodology or results [@mishra2026correction]. Ha Thanh's TAN-IDS frames transfer-aware, deployment-oriented evaluation around NetFlow representation and adaptation scenarios [@hathanh2026tanids]. Buriboyev reports a factorial protocol audit on CICIDS2017 involving temporal partitioning, shortcut-related factors, protocol interactions, and ranking instability, but its Zenodo record and linked article copy do not independently establish peer-reviewed publication status [@buriboyev2026protocol]. These studies make clear that the relevant evaluation dimensions and several of their combinations are prior art.

## 1.3 Specific empirical gap

The remaining problem is narrower: how should a linked, source-faithful chain of study-specific evidence be interpreted when its evaluation contracts change across population, chronology, threshold, representation, transfer direction, prevalence, hardware, attack-family eligibility, and model realization? This is not a gap created by the absence of temporal validation, semantic alignment, explainability, LOAO, or reproducibility practice. It is an interpretation problem concerning which observations survive, which become conditional, which planned cells are unavailable, and which claims must stop at the artifact boundary.

This study addresses that problem through a bounded audit centered on CSE-CIC-IDS2018 and a restricted CICIDS2017 bridge. The processed and stratified reference remains a conventional baseline. Separately governed source-faithful temporal populations, frozen operating points, registered feature and placebo controls, direction-specific semantic bridges, analytic prevalence/workload translations, component-level profiling, eligible LOAO, and finite-seed closure are treated as different estimands. The resulting contribution is the documented empirical pattern and its interpretation ceilings, not the invention of a new evaluation framework. Numerical findings are deferred to the Results and are not used here to imply portability beyond the evaluated contracts.

## 1.4 Research questions

The audit addresses six questions:

1. **Benchmark and forward validity:** How does discrimination under the processed CSE-CIC-IDS2018 reference relate to ranking on the separately governed forward target?
2. **Ranking and decision transfer:** Do ranking changes under random versus chronological development transfer to the frozen operating points selected during development?
3. **Representation and explanation sensitivity:** How do registered feature subsets, split geometry, learner realization, TreeSHAP comparison, and LIME fidelity change the interpretation of feature reliance?
4. **Directional transfer:** Does ranking transfer symmetrically between CSE-CIC-IDS2018 and CICIDS2017 under the frozen bridge62 and bridge70 contracts?
5. **Operational and computational interpretation:** How do assumed prevalence, workload, cost, and the measured component-level hardware boundary alter the meaning of discrimination and threshold results?
6. **Eligibility and realization stability:** What behavior remains under eligible leave-one-attack-family-out evaluation and the registered finite-seed/control program?

Each question is answered only by the frozen stages mapped in the crosswalk supplied with this section. None requires a new fit, target opening, threshold search, statistical test, or scientific recomputation.

## 1.5 Contributions

The study makes three linked, bounded contributions.

**1. Protocol-conditioned empirical findings.** The audit reports source-faithful temporal and frozen-operating-point results, registered shortcut and placebo controls, finite-seed stability, and direction-specific semantic transfer. The relevant Stage 29 claims are `CLM29-005`, `CLM29-008`–`CLM29-011`, and `CLM29-016`, supported by `E29-016`, `E29-020`–`E29-027`, and `E29-037`–`E29-040`. Chronological development ranked higher on the shared forward target across the five registered realizations, but frozen thresholds transferred poorly; shortcut effects varied by split and learner; transfer was directional. The chronology/family-composition confounding, historical target reuse, K79 exact-identity boundary, cancelled `GROUNDED_S4` cells, and failure to isolate one causal mechanism prevent claims of universal temporal superiority, concept drift, causal shortcut explanation, or symmetric portability.

**2. Operational and eligibility-gated stress tests.** The study complements those findings with analytic prevalence/workload projections, frozen operating-point utility, component-level profiling, and eligible-family LOAO. Claims `CLM29-013`–`CLM29-015`, supported by `E29-028`–`E29-039`, show that PPV, alert burden, yield, cost, measured computation, and withheld-family behavior can diverge. Profiling covers compatible prepared-input-to-probability paths on recorded hardware and software; unsupported paths remain unsupported. LOAO excludes DOS and AUTH_BRUTE_FORCE, while Infiltration has 36 held-out positives and remains descriptive; known-category `Infilteration` support of 3,967 is a different population. These results are analytic or benchmark-bound evidence, not SOC observations, end-to-end latency, universal zero-day detection, or production readiness.

**3. Evidence-governed interpretation.** Claim-to-artifact provenance, target-opening records, incomplete historical reproducibility, cancelled-cell governance, and unsupported-path statuses make the evidence boundary inspectable. `CLM29-012` and `CLM29-014`–`CLM29-016`, together with `LIM29-001`–`LIM29-018`, document what is directly demonstrated, supported, descriptive, cancelled, unavailable, or unsupported. This is a transparency contribution built on established reproducibility practice, not a wholly new methodology or independent rerun. Static and scalar verification do not imply complete end-to-end regeneration.
