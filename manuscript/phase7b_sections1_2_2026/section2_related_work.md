# 2. Related Work

## 2.1 Benchmark Protocols and Temporal Validity

Benchmark datasets remain valuable because they make model comparisons possible under shared labels, features, and evaluation procedures. Surveys of network-based intrusion-detection datasets also show why those comparisons are conditional on collection design, labeling, representation, and class construction [@ring2019survey]. The CSE-CIC-IDS2018 and CICIDS2017 releases provide important flow-derived resources [@sharafaldin2018toward; @cse_cic_ids2018_official; @cicids2017_official], but a processed or rebalanced table should not be treated as a natural deployment population. Imbalanced-learning guidance and precision-recall analysis further motivate reporting native quantities appropriate to the evaluated class distribution [@he2009imbalanced; @saito2015precision].

Security-dataset audits have documented extraction errors, duplicate-like relationships, and collection artifacts that complicate random partitioning [@engelen2021troubleshooting; @arp2022dos]. Time-aware evaluation addresses a different question: whether a model trained under an earlier role can rank a later population. Work on experimental bias across space and time shows that partition geometry can change reported results [@pendlebury2019tesseract]. Yet chronology can also change attack-family composition and prevalence. A chronological contrast therefore identifies behavior under a chosen forward contract; it does not, on its own, prove concept drift or establish that chronological development is universally superior.

The most direct recent comparisons are Berhili et al. and Biswas. Berhili et al. study protocol-dependent accuracy inflation across UNSW-NB15, NSL-KDD, and CICIDS2017. Their accessible implementation and package document validation-split comparisons, seed sensitivity, preprocessing leakage, semantically aligned transfer, calibration and threshold analyses, statistical testing, and reproducibility artifacts [@berhili2026protocol; @berhili2026reproducibility]. The article and supplement were not fully accessible for this review; consequently, dimensions not independently inspected remain unverified rather than absent. Biswas's 2026 Preprints.org record is explicitly a non-peer-reviewed preprint [@biswas2026drift]. It examines CSE-CIC-IDS2018 under random, class-stratified temporal, and class-blind chronological protocols, then studies cross-regime transfer and target calibration with an unseen-family framing. Its temporal-protocol evidence makes the conceptual problem directly prior to the present study. Buriboyev likewise reports a four-factor CICIDS2017 protocol audit with temporal partitioning, shortcut-related factors, interactions, and ranking instability, but Zenodo resource metadata and a linked article copy do not independently verify peer-reviewed venue status [@buriboyev2026protocol].

The present audit therefore does not claim to introduce temporal validation or protocol-bias analysis. Its narrower empirical question is how the frozen CSE-CIC-IDS2018 development contracts behave on one source-faithful forward target when ranking and operating-point transfer are reported separately, when feature interventions and controls are retained, and when finite-seed stability is distinguished from causal or population inference. The K79 control establishes exact corrected-second-plus-predictor identity under its declared rule; it does not establish session, endpoint, source-IP, five-tuple, or S4 independence. The temporal target was historically opened and later reused for registered stability analysis, so that analysis is not a new blind holdout.

## 2.2 Feature Semantics, Shortcut Sensitivity, and Explainability

A detector can exploit fields that are predictive in one collection protocol without encoding behavior that transports to another. Dataset troubleshooting and broader computer-security evaluation guidance therefore motivate inspection of identifiers, extraction fields, collection artifacts, and feature dependencies [@engelen2021troubleshooting; @arp2022dos]. Cross-dataset work reinforces the importance of a semantic feature contract: shared names or positional overlap are not sufficient evidence that two variables represent the same construct [@dhooge2020interdataset; @layeghy2023crossdomain].

Feature ablation is useful but limited. Removing a registered field or subset measures sensitivity to that intervention under the tested split and learner. It does not prove that the field is leakage, that it caused transfer failure, or that the observed mechanism is causal. Matched-size placebos, stump probes, proxy-absorption checks, and family-support records can constrain interpretations, but observational controls may still leave multiple mechanisms compatible with the result. The present study follows this boundary: its shortcut claims are conditional sensitivity findings, and its Stage 28 controls did not isolate one causal temporal or family-composition mechanism.

Explanation methods provide another, distinct diagnostic. LIME constructs a local surrogate explanation for a prediction [@ribeiro2016lime]. SHAP formalizes additive feature attribution, while TreeSHAP provides a tree-specific implementation [@lundberg2017shap; @lundberg2020trees]. Research on explanation infidelity and sensitivity cautions that stable or plausible attributions need not be faithful to the underlying decision function [@yeh2019infidelity]. Accordingly, explanation stability, model-decision reproduction, local surrogate fidelity, and agreement between attribution rankings are separate properties.

The present audit uses that separation. TreeSHAP agreement across the two tree learners is descriptive attribution evidence. The deterministic LIME panel tests local behavior against explicit criteria; repeatability across perturbation seeds does not override local decision failures. Integrated Gradients and attention analyses remain bounded to their documented neural-model contexts and are not generalized into a common explanation for all branches. The empirical contribution is therefore a documented representation-and-explanation sensitivity pattern under registered subsets and criteria, not a new explainability method or causal account of shortcut use.

## 2.3 Cross-Dataset Transfer and Operational Evaluation

Cross-dataset IDS studies show that transfer depends on dataset pairing, representation, preprocessing, source-to-target direction, and the target population [@dhooge2020interdataset; @layeghy2023crossdomain; @cantone2024crossdataset]. Elangovan et al. extend this line with temporal and semantic generalization across four public datasets, including forward and cross-domain comparisons [@elangovan2026crossdataset]. Mishra et al. study harmonized cross-dataset multi-model learning with statistical validation [@mishra2026harmonized]. The correction associated with that article concerns its funding statement and is not a methodology or results correction [@mishra2026correction]. Ha Thanh's TAN-IDS frames transfer in a unified NetFlow representation with adaptation and deployment-oriented scenarios [@hathanh2026tanids]. Adaptation-oriented evaluation and source-only transfer answer different questions; neither is treated here as generally superior.

Berhili et al. provide especially close prior evidence by documenting semantic alignment, bidirectional transfer, and deployable-versus-oracle calibration in their accessible implementation [@berhili2026protocol]. Biswas reports cross-regime zero-shot transfer and target calibration in the non-peer-reviewed preprint record [@biswas2026drift]. These studies prevent a claim that semantic alignment, cross-dataset transfer, calibration, or operating-point analysis is newly invented. The present study instead preserves source and target roles under frozen bridge contracts. Bridge62 and bridge70 are not interchangeable: bridge62 excludes the eight aggregate flag-count fields, whereas bridge70 retains the aggregate fields and compares published with corrected serialization. The two directions are reported separately, not averaged into a portability score. The two planned `GROUNDED_S4` cells were cancelled before target opening because durable physical-row membership could not be recovered without a new heuristic; they are no-result administrative conditions, not negative model findings.

Operational interpretation begins with the base-rate problem. Sensitivity and specificity do not determine positive predictive value when attacks are rare [@axelsson2000baserate]. Prior-probability-shift methods can translate classifier outputs to new class priors, but their interpretation depends on assumptions about stable class-conditional behavior [@saerens2002prior]. Controlled human-factors work also demonstrates that false-alarm rate can affect analyst performance and time on task [@layman2023falsealarms]. Thus PPV, alert volume, analyst-hours, detection yield, capacity, and cost should not be collapsed into one favorable metric. The present Stage25 quantities are deterministic projections under fixed TPR/FPR, assumed prevalence, traffic volume, service time, analyst capacity, and relative costs. They are not observed SOC workload, organization-specific economics, or production utility.

## 2.4 Computational Feasibility, Unseen-Family Evaluation, and Reproducibility

Computational results are meaningful only at a declared boundary. Benchmarking practice emphasizes that timing depends on implementation, hardware, batch, and measurement procedure [@mattson2020mlperf]. Online IDS systems also illustrate the importance of distinguishing feature extraction and stream processing from model inference [@mirsky2018kitsune]. The present component-level profiling begins with prepared model input and ends with materialized probabilities on recorded CPU/Tesla-T4 environments. Packet capture, flow extraction, feature construction, representation conversion, alert aggregation, transport, and analyst response are outside the timed boundary. Unsupported backends, timeouts, and resource-limited paths remain statuses; they are not converted into poor timings. The results therefore establish measured compatibility and feasibility for the recorded paths, not end-to-end latency or hardware-independent architecture superiority.

Unknown-attack evaluation has established conceptual links with open-set recognition and supervised, unsupervised, and meta-learning approaches to unknown attacks [@scheirer2013openset; @zoppi2023unknown; @shin2023openset]. In IDS, however, “unseen” is protocol-specific. A valid LOAO condition requires zero exposure to the held-out family in training and validation, a declared chronology, sufficient target support, and a clear distinction between ranking and threshold behavior. The present eligibility gates exclude DOS and AUTH_BRUTE_FORCE; Infiltration is executable but descriptive with 36 held-out positives. This must not be conflated with the known-category `Infilteration` support of 3,967 in the processed reference. The resulting evidence is selective family- and learner-dependent behavior, not universal zero-day detection.

Statistical and realization controls answer different questions. Conditional bootstrap intervals describe uncertainty for frozen populations and arrays; five registered seeds describe finite model-realization stability. Neither becomes a population guarantee merely by being reported together. The Stage28 ordering is descriptive over five seeds, and family-level behavior remains heterogeneous.

Reproducibility work emphasizes code, experimental detail, and regeneration [@pineau2021reproducibility]. Berhili's package is a relevant example: it supplies code, scripts, indices, seeds, and result files, while datasets remain external [@berhili2026reproducibility]. The present evidence-governance layer should be read in that context. Claim-to-artifact mappings, target-opening records, equivalence receipts, cancellation records, and unsupported-path statuses make this study's evidence boundaries inspectable. They do not constitute a wholly new reproducibility methodology or prove complete rerun equivalence. Historical source data, models, arrays, and environments remain heterogeneous in availability; static verification and deterministic scalar checks are not the same as full scientific regeneration.

Taken together, prior work establishes the relevant evaluation tools and many close protocol questions. The present paper is positioned more narrowly as a source-faithful empirical validity audit of CSE-CIC-IDS2018 and a restricted CICIDS2017 bridge. Its empirical positioning rests on the particular frozen observations and interpretation ceilings reported across temporal ranking, operating-point transfer, feature and explanation sensitivity, directional transfer, analytic workload, measured computation, eligible-family behavior, and evidence status. These observations are bounded by the stated populations, bridges, supports, historical target reuse, finite seeds, cancelled cells, and incomplete reproducibility; they do not establish methodological priority or universal IDS behavior.
