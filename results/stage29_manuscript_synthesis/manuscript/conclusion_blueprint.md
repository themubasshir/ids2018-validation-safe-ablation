# Conclusion blueprint

The conclusion should answer one question: which conventional benchmark inferences survived increasingly realistic validation, which did not, and what evaluation discipline follows?

- **Survived within scope:** strong discrimination under the frozen processed reference condition (CLM29-001); a five-seed chronological-over-random forward ranking direction on the shared historical target (CLM29-008); meaningful forward bridge ranking (CLM29-011); selected eligible-family patterns across learners/seeds (CLM29-015); measured computational feasibility on specific compatible paths (CLM29-014).
- **Did not survive uniformly:** frozen threshold transfer (CLM29-009); symmetric cross-dataset transport (CLM29-011); a universal shortcut-removal effect (CLM29-010); low-prevalence operational suitability (CLM29-013); uniform unseen-family behavior (CLM29-015).
- **Remained unresolved or bounded:** one causal drift/shortcut mechanism (CLM29-016); cancelled Stage24 cells (CLM29-012); ineligible and low-support families; field SOC utility; hardware-independent timing; complete full-rerun reproducibility.
- **Evaluation discipline:** treat benchmark, chronology, shortcuts, domain transfer, prevalence, compute, novelty, and realization stability as separate axes; report native metrics and contradictions; lock target access/adaptation; disclose support and cancellations; bind claims and numbers to provenance.

Do not conclude that “ML-based IDS does not work,” that the literature is invalid, or that supervised IDS cannot detect novel attacks. End with an evidence-bounded methodological recommendation, not a universal verdict.
