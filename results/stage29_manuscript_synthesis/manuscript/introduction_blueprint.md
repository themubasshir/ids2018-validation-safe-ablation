# Introduction blueprint

1. **Inference at issue.** Benchmark metrics are useful, but a high score on a processed split is not by itself evidence of chronological, cross-dataset, operational, computational, or unseen-family validity (CLM29-001).
2. **Validity breaks.** Briefly motivate split geometry and duplicate roles, temporal ordering, shortcut-prone cues, domain bridges, base rates, threshold transfer, runtime constraints, and novelty support. Do not reveal all results here.
3. **Fragmentation gap.** Existing repository references may motivate that these dimensions are commonly studied separately; do not conduct or imply a new literature search in Stage29.
4. **Connected framework.** Present the eight-axis validation chain and the principle that heterogeneous native metrics are not collapsed into one degradation or robustness score.
5. **Study design overview.** Introduce the conventional reference condition, progressively stronger stress tests, protocol locking, shared frozen targets, and final seed/control closure. Historical stages appear only as provenance underneath components.
6. **Contribution preview.** Use the six items in `final_contribution_list.md`, shortened to one sentence each.
7. **Claim discipline.** State that results are bounded to evaluated datasets, feature bridges, prior-shift scenarios, measured hardware, eligible families, and frozen seeds.

Avoid proposed-model framing, “the model generalizes,” “zero-day detection,” universal IDS-failure rhetoric, and architecture-superiority claims.
