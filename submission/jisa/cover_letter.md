# Cover Letter

**To:** Editor-in-Chief, *Journal of Information Security and Applications*

**Re:** *A Multi-Axis Empirical Audit of Machine-Learning Intrusion Detection*

Dear Editor,

Please consider this manuscript for publication as a Research Article in the *Journal of Information Security and Applications*.

The manuscript addresses a practical security-evaluation problem: strong intrusion-detection benchmark scores do not, by themselves, establish validity across time, representations, datasets, rare-event prevalence, operating points, deployment boundaries, or attack families withheld from development. Rather than proposing another IDS architecture, the study conducts a governed empirical audit across these questions using native metrics, frozen operating rules, explicit populations, controls, and claim-to-artifact provenance.

The contribution is fourfold. First, the study separates temporal validation, operating-point transfer, shortcut and placebo controls, bidirectional cross-dataset transfer, prevalence/workload projections, hardware-specific profiling, eligible leave-one-attack-family-out evaluation, and realization stability. Second, it maps claims and numerical values to evidence and preserves the distinction between static verification and complete rerunning. Third, it reports heterogeneous findings without collapsing them into a single robustness score: chronological development retained forward ranking across five registered seeds, but frozen thresholds transferred poorly; cross-dataset ranking was directional; projected PPV and analyst workload diverged; hardware advantage depended on the path; and held-out-family behavior varied by learner and support. Fourth, it provides a study-derived checklist linking questions, metrics, controls, and interpretation ceilings.

These conditional and negative findings are directly relevant to applied information-security research. They identify where benchmark capability survived, where it became conditional, and where the evidence does not support a broader claim. The manuscript preserves reproducibility restrictions, closed-data boundaries, cancelled analyses, low-support and ineligible families, and the measured hardware boundary rather than presenting an artificial completeness claim.

The work is original and is not under consideration elsewhere. Any prior repository or related-publication relationship is disclosed in the accompanying declarations. All authors will review and approve the final submission and author list before upload.
